PICO 2 EA888 DASHBOARD: PROPOSED WIRING AND INSPECTION NET LIST
Revision: 2026-10-11. No assembled board, Longan revision, ECU, or vehicle
connection was inspected for this revision of the document.

Parts named by the existing project: Raspberry Pi Pico 2, Pimoroni PIM715
Display Pack 2.8, Adafruit 5880 I2C encoder, Longan 1030002 OBD-II Serial
CAN Bus Dev Kit, Adafruit 3218 latching switch, and Pololu 3782 D36V28F5
5 V regulator. Check markings on the actual assembled parts before wiring.

POWER NETS
===========

OBD-II pin 16 (+vehicle supply)
  -> suitably rated fuse and verified automotive input protection
  -> latching switch
  -> Pololu VIN.

OBD-II pins 4/5, Pololu GND, Pico GND, Longan GND
  -> common ground, with layout and conductor ratings checked in hardware.

Pololu VOUT (regulated 5 V, switched domain)
  -> 5 V distribution and the specified PTC protection
  -> Pico 2 VSYS and Longan Serial CAN module VCC on versions requiring 5 V.

This direct-VSYS net is for vehicle power with USB DISCONNECTED. Before
USB programming, disconnect the regulator/Longan 5 V distribution from
Pico VSYS and isolate the adapter UART connection as required to prevent
an unpowered adapter being driven through its signal pins.

USB VBUS feeds VSYS through the Pico's on-board D1 diode. With the net above
still connected, USB can power Longan and drive the disabled regulator's
output even when the vehicle switch is off. Switch-off isolation therefore
does not hold in that configuration. Pico 2 datasheet section 5.5 specifies
an additional external Schottky diode or its P-channel MOSFET alternative
for two-source power ORing. Keep Longan on the switched side of any such
isolation; verify reverse-current blocking and UART power sequencing on
the actual circuit before permitting simultaneous USB and vehicle power.
No dual-source circuit has been specified or validated for this assembly.

The Longan 1030002 kit contains a Serial CAN module and an OBD connector.
Its module VCC is not a 12 V VIN input. Do not connect OBD-II pin 16 or the
switched vehicle supply directly to module VCC. Route its supply from the
switched regulated rail so the adapter turns off with the dashboard. Longan
documents 5 V VCC for V1.0-V1.2. V1.3 supports 5 V or 3.3 V VCC; a 3.3 V
option also requires a checked Pico 3.3 V rail budget and physical revision
identification. The actual kit PCB revision is presently unknown.

Leave the Pololu EN pin disconnected from Pico GPIO. Pololu pulls EN to VIN
through 100 kohm; a direct connection can expose an unpowered/reset Pico
pin to vehicle supply. An optional MCU shutdown needs a separate, rated
transistor/open-drain interface and a defined latch/wake circuit. Cutting
regulator power also cuts the MCU's ability to hold EN low. No such circuit
is specified or validated here.

The previously proposed 3 A blade fuse, SS34 series diode, SMAJ14A TVS,
500 mA PTC, and 100 uF/100 nF decoupling are provisional components, not a
validated automotive surge or wiring protection design. Check fuse against
wire gauge and fault energy, TVS/clamp behavior, polarity, regulator input
range, PTC trip/hold behavior, and actual supply transients before fitting.

CAN AND UART NETS
=================

OBD-II pin 6 -> Longan CAN-H; pin 14 -> Longan CAN-L. Verify the connector
cable and module labels by continuity before connection. The module is a
UART-to-raw-CAN bridge, not an ELM327 interface. The optional longan-rx12
profile instantiates UART0 on GP0/GP1. Its adapter/vehicle contract remains
unqualified: no diagnostic CAN requests are enabled. Wiring is untested.

Longan UART TX -> appropriate logic-level interface -> Pico GP1 (UART0 RX).
Pico GP0 (UART0 TX) -> appropriate logic-level interface -> Longan UART RX.
Share ground. Use a translator appropriate for push-pull UART signals where
translation is needed; do not assume a bidirectional I2C level shifter is
qualified for the chosen baud rate.

Longan version / VCC / UART condition:
  V1.0:       5 V VCC, 5 V IO; translate Longan TX before Pico RX.
  V1.1/V1.2: 5 V VCC; 3.3 V IO needs the documented solder-pad option.
  V1.3:       5 V or 3.3 V VCC; documented 3.3 V IO option.

Measure TX idle/high and low levels at the chosen VCC and setting before
connecting either UART line. Pico GPIO is 3.3 V logic. Longan documents
9600 baud by default and up to 115200. The implemented optional profile
requires an externally preprovisioned, verified 115200 8N1 adapter. Firmware
does not scan baud rates, negotiate a change, or write adapter EEPROM.

The old 1030002 kit page describes a 120 ohm CAN resistor connected by
default; the current 1030001 module page describes soldering a pad to add
termination. Inspect and measure the actual PCB. A diagnostic tap on an
already terminated vehicle bus normally must not add a third 120 ohm end
resistor. Confirm CAN-H/CAN-L idle and bus resistance with the vehicle and
adapter unpowered as appropriate for the installation procedure.

PICO 2 / PIM715 PIN MAP
======================

The table is for the non-wireless Pico 2 with PIM715 Display Pack 2.8.
"Header spare" means present on the Pico 2's 40-pin headers and not used
by this firmware or an active named pack function with the optional PIM715
SP/CE expansion unpopulated. Access and shared connector traces in an
assembled stack still need inspection. PIM715 RGB traces can be cut, but
are assumed intact here. The optional te-trace profile enables TEON 35h/00h
and records software-observed rising edges on GP21. Physical timing remains
unverified; TE is not a panel-presence or watchdog-health assertion.

GPIO       Allocation or physical condition                  Category
---------- ------------------------------------------------- ----------------
GP0-GP1    Optional UART0 to Longan serial bridge           Optional, header
GP2-GP3    No named connection                               Header spare
GP4-GP5    PIM715 Qw/ST I2C0 to Adafruit 5880 encoder       Pack connection
GP6-GP11   No named PIM715 2.8 function or firmware use     Header spare
GP12-GP15  PIM715 buttons A, B, X, Y                        Pack connection
GP16       PIM715 LCD data/command                          Pack connection
GP17       PIM715 LCD chip select                           Pack connection
GP18       PIM715 LCD SPI0 clock                            Pack connection
GP19       PIM715 LCD SPI0 transmit                         Pack connection
GP20       PIM715 AP2502 backlight enable PWM               Pack connection
GP21       PIM715 LCD tearing-effect output                 Optional TE input
GP22       No named connection                               Header spare
GP23       On-board SMPS power-save control                 Not on header
GP24       On-board VBUS sense                               Not on header
GP25       On-board user LED                                 Not on header
GP26-GP28  PIM715 2.8 RGB LED (active low, traces intact)   Pack connection

The old GP6/GP7/GP8 RGB assignment belongs to other Display Pack variants.
GP21 is not free: it carries LCD TE. GP23-GP25 are not general 40-pin-header
GPIOs on Pico 2. Candidate SPI1 pins GP8/GP9/GP10/GP11 and interrupt GP22
have no RGB conflict on PIM715 2.8, but using an alternative SPI CAN board
would require a separate electrical and firmware design, including physical
clearance and trace inspection. The currently named Longan device uses UART.

BACKLIGHT TIMING
================

The PIM715 schematic names AP2502 for backlight control. Its datasheet
permits PWM to 150 kHz and specifies EN-high pulse time of at least 1 us.
Firmware now derives a 1 kHz output from the configured system clock using
TOP=9999 and dividers 15/25/28/29/30 for the 150/250/280/290/300 MHz
profiles. Off drives compare=0. The old 45%-100% visible-duty mapping is
retained until a scope and photometric/visual check establish useful lower
duty settings. The clock and rate calculations are host tested; the actual
GP20 waveform, dimming range and flicker remain unmeasured.

POWER BUDGET AND PRE-CONNECTION RECORD
======================================

The old 90 mA vehicle and 150 mA 5 V totals excluded Longan module current
and were estimates. Measure Pico/display, encoder, Longan, switch LED, and
regulator input currents in each operating mode, including overclock,
backlight off/full, startup, and adapter active. Recheck regulator thermal
margin and protection components from measured totals.

Record the actual Longan PCB revision and UART VCC/IO setting; PIM715 and
Pico 2 revisions; CAN termination state; wiring continuity; 5 V and 3.3 V
rails; UART high/low levels; EN-to-Pico isolation; and GP20 frequency and
minimum high pulse at stock and each selected overclock. Device behavior,
ECU support, and physical frame rate are still unverified.
